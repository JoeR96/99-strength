# 99 Strength

A phone-first training planner. It runs your whole week from one screen: lifting on Greg Nuckols'
Average to Savage 2 (A2S2) programme, cardio, mobility, daily habits and body-weight checkpoints.
At the gym you log your session in 99 Strength, it sends the workout to Hevy, and it works out what comes next.

Live at **https://99-strength.enjoeneer.dev**. Install it to your home screen (steps below).

## What it does

**Workout** (the first tab, the **Current workout** page) follows your training cycle, not the calendar. It
always shows the next session to do: "Week 1 · Session 2 of 4", Lower A, each exercise with its sets and
weights (or **Set TM** while a main lift has no training max), the cardio finisher as the last item with its
prescription ("5 min continuous @ level 6, 2 more sessions to step up"), the warm-up, an estimated time and a
big **Start workout**. Finish a session and the page moves straight on to the next one. Under it: the home
routine the plan pairs with that session ("After this workout: Mobility B, Play"), the rest of this week's
sessions and **Preview next week** (tap any session to see it set by set, read-only), and today's habits and
weigh-in (only while you have a weigh-in habit). A session you just finished shows **In Hevy ✓** or **Not in
Hevy yet** with **Retry**. The full week view and history are one link away.

**Mobility** (second tab) lists your routines by kind (warm-ups, corrective, stretch, yoga flow,
follow-along). Each shows its minutes and drill count; **Preview** shows every pose with its dose, cues
and the easier and harder versions, and **Play** starts it.

**Cardio** (from Progress, `/cardio`) holds your walk, run and finisher tracks, the running gate and the
week's zone 4–5 warning.

**Plan** is the weekly schedule plus phases (cut, maintenance, pause, deload) and weight checkpoints.
Pausing for a holiday freezes the programme, and missed sessions during a pause aren't counted as failures.

**Lifting** follows A2S2: a 21-week cycle of three 7-week blocks, each ending in a deload. The last set of
a main lift is done for as many reps as possible (AMRAP), and your result there moves the training max up or down.
Accessories progress on their own rules: add a set, add weight, or drop back when you miss.

**Cardio** has three protocols built in: Norwegian 4×4 intervals, Couch to 5K (with "repeat this week"),
and steady easy work. Each session logs its duration, effort, and minutes in heart-rate zones 4–5. The zone minutes count toward a weekly target.

**Mobility** routines are ordered lists of drills, each with sets, reps, seconds or breaths. A drill can
add an extra set for one side and list a standing alternative for when you can't get on the floor. You can
rename a routine, change its target minutes or delete it. A routine that's still in your week can't be deleted
until you take it out of the schedule, and sessions you've already done stay in your history.

Each routine has a kind: warm-up, corrective, stretch, yoga flow or follow-along video. Yoga flows and videos
go to Hevy as Yoga, the rest as Stretching. Drills done on each side always start on the left.

A drill can have levels, such as Easier / Standard / Harder, or a ladder like the reverse plank (level 1
tabletop with knees bent, level 2 hands on a bench, level 3 full reverse plank). Each level has its own sets,
amount, cues and an optional range, like holding 15 to 30 seconds in 5-second steps. The player shows the
level you're on and its cues, and the easier or harder version if you ask. When you finish, each drill is
marked "felt clean" unless you tap "strained". After two clean sessions in a row the hold goes up a step, and
at the top of the range the app suggests the next level, which you can accept or turn down. A strain takes the
hold back a step. Before you start a routine, its Progress section shows each drill's level and hold, any suggested
level with **Accept** or **Not yet**, and the last 10 changes with their reasons, with **Undo last change**. The
same Accept / Not yet appears on the finish screen when a session earns a new level.

A **follow-along** routine is just a title, a link and the minutes it usually takes. Start opens the video in a
new tab; when you come back, "I'm back — log it" records the minutes you actually did.

**Habits** come in three types: counters (water 3/6), numbers (weight) and checkboxes. A counter can have a step, so one tap adds 25 g of protein instead of 1. Each habit has a minimum
for bad days, and the streak uses a "never miss twice" rule: one missed day keeps it, two in a row breaks it.

**Progress** shows your weight trend against the checkpoints, plus hard sets per muscle group this
week against your caps.

**Meals and shopping list** let you pick the week's meals and get a combined shopping list to share or copy.

## Gym mode

At the gym, open the app and tap **Start workout**. You get one list for the whole
session: tick the warm-up drills (or tap **All done**), then tap each set as you finish it. The weight
and reps are already filled in from your plan; tap the pencil if a set went differently. The last set of a
main lift asks how many reps you got. If the day has a finisher, log the minutes, level, floors and how it
felt (Easy, Solid or Brutal), then tap **Finish**. **Finish early** saves only what you ticked; anything
you skipped keeps its progression for next time.

A rest timer starts after every set and beeps when it ends, and the screen stays on. Everything you tick
is saved on your phone, so it works with no signal and finishes when you are back online. If you
finish the same day on two devices, the second one says **This day was already finished on another
device**, changes nothing, and lets you throw away that device's copy.

When you finish, the session counts in the app straight away and the workout is sent to Hevy for you.
If Hevy can't be reached, the Workout page shows **Not in Hevy yet** with a **Retry** button, and the app keeps
trying in the background. It never adds the same workout to Hevy twice. Choose each lifting day's warm-up
and finisher under **Settings → Gym sessions** (a stair track, a cardio protocol, or a simple
"Treadmill, easy incline, 10 min" cool-down you just tick off).

Supersets show as one block (2a/2b): do a set of A, then a set of B, then rest. One-side exercises list the
left side first. If a main lift has no training max yet, it shows reps and % only and asks you to **Set
your training max**: type it in, or enter a recent set (weight × reps) and the app works it out (about 90 %
of your estimated one-rep max).

## Plan templates

**Plan templates** (in Settings next to Programmes, on the Programmes page, or on the Workout page when you have no plan) lists the ready-made plans:
"Lumbridge Lard Shed" (a cut to 85 kg by March 2027) and "Plan v3 — Base/Build/Transition". Each shows its phases with dates, the week, every
session's exercises, warm-ups, home routines, cardio tracks and what's left to fill in. Pick a start date
(it moves to a Monday), enter training maxes now or leave them for later, and apply. If you already have a
plan, the app asks first; your old plan and its history are kept. Applying the plan you already have offers a
**fresh copy** (the current copy is kept, inactive, with its history) or keeping the current one. Everything it creates is yours to edit.

Plan v3 runs A2S2 on bench, incline Smith press and front squat; the pause squat is reps per set (4 × 5–8, weight
set after the first session). Exercises are straight sets at normal rest (no supersets). There is no Walk builder
track (walks stay a habit); Walk → run waits behind its own gate: trend weight at or under 90 kg and no knee, shin
or Achilles pain for four weeks.

To rename your plan, tap the pencil next to its name at the top of Progress, type the new name and tap Save.

## How it connects

| Integration | Direction | What flows |
|---|---|---|
| **Hevy** | both ways | Each scheduled session is pushed to Hevy as a routine, with warm-up drills first. Sessions you finish in gym mode are sent to Hevy as finished workouts. Finished Hevy workouts are pulled back and matched to the planned session, and AMRAP results drive the A2S2 progression as if you'd entered them by hand. A workout that matches nothing goes to an inbox, where one tap assigns it. Weigh-ins are mirrored to Hevy. Needs a Hevy Pro API key, which the server stores encrypted. The browser never sees it. |
| **Push notifications** | server → phone | "Time to train" at the session's start time, and an evening nudge when a habit is still under its minimum. Uses standard Web Push. On iPhone it only works from the installed home-screen app (iOS 16.4+). |
| **Desktop widgets and scripts** | both ways, limited | Personal API tokens (`nns_pat_…`, created in Settings) can read and log habits and weigh-ins and read the plan for any day (today's sessions, and the coming days with each lift's planned sets), and follow a live change feed, and nothing else. The Terran CC habit widget uses one. |
| **Live updates** | server → app and widgets | Tick a habit in Terran CC and the open app updates within a second or two, and the other way round. Coming back to the app also refreshes habits and today. If the live feed is down, nothing breaks: the app just refreshes on its usual triggers. |
| **Sign-in** | — | Clerk. |
| **Offline** | — | The app shell is cached. Habit taps, weigh-ins, finished sessions, cardio track logs and how a routine's drills felt, made without signal are queued on the phone and synced later, each one dated to the day it was made and counted once even if it is sent twice. A track waiting to sync says so. If you leave a routine or a track's settings half edited, the app keeps your changes on the phone and offers **Restore unsaved changes?** next time, and asks before you navigate away. |

Not connected, by design: MacroFactor (it has no usable API) and calorie or protein tracking.

## Cardio tracks

Cardio now progresses on its own, the way your lifts do. Open **Progress → Cardio tracks** (or
`/cardio-tracks`) and start a track from one of three templates. Every number in a template can
be changed afterwards in the track's **Info** sheet.

- **Walk builder**: easy treadmill walking. It starts at 20 minutes and adds 5 minutes at a time up to 45. After that it keeps you at 45 minutes and raises the incline by 1 % at a time, up to 10 %.
- **Walk → run**: Couch to 5K over 9 weeks, two runs a week. You move to the next week after 3 good sessions. After week 9, a "5 K builder" makes your continuous run about 10 % longer at a time, up to 35 minutes. Treadmill or grass is best.
- **Stair finisher**: a hard finish after an upper-body lifting day, with 3 easy minutes either side (level 3) so you spend time in the lower zones too. The hard part starts at 5 minutes of steady climbing at level 6 and adds a minute every two good sessions, up to 15. Then the level goes up one and you drop back to 10 minutes, not 5. After that come 1 minute hard / 1 minute easy intervals, from 6 rounds to 10; at 10 the hard level goes up one and you drop back to 8. Only the hard part counts towards stepping up. In the track's **Info** sheet you can change the warm-up and cool-down minutes and level (0 turns one off) and where the minutes drop back to after a level.

**Logging takes one tap.** Pick **Easy**, **Solid** or **Brutal** (saved as RPE 4, 7 or 9) and
you're done. Under **More details** you can add minutes, the machine level or incline, floors,
heart rate, minutes in zone 4–5, an exact RPE, or a pain flag (a niggle, or "had to stop", plus
where it hurts).

**How a track moves:**
- A session counts when you did it as prescribed, you had no pain, and either it wasn't Brutal (on easy tracks) or you spent at least half the work time in zone 4–5 (on hard tracks). Heart rate is optional. If you log no heart rate, the app goes by your effort instead.
- Two good sessions move you up one step. Two missed sessions in a row move you back one step.
- If it wants to repeat a week, drop back a week, or move you to the next stage, it asks first. You can say yes or carry on.
- **Undo** puts the track back exactly as it was before your last log, decision or edit. **Pause** keeps a track where it is while you're ill or away.
- Each track has a check-in test every 4 weeks (for example floors in 5 minutes, or your 5 K time). Your best results are kept.

The Cardio page shows each track's session and how many more good sessions you need before it steps up.
It also warns you when your zone 4–5 minutes for the week go over your plan's maximum. Progress
shows a chart for each track, with every change and the reason for it, and the same data as a
table.

Walks also tick your **Walks** habit, if you have one, so you don't have to log them twice.
Set your max heart rate and zones in **Settings → Heart rate**. The zones start as WHOOP's
50/60/70/80/90 % of max, and you can change them. Without a max heart rate, tracks go by your
effort alone.

**Weeks of Couch to 5K follow your plan.** If your plan has two runs a week for the track, you
still need three good sessions before the next week. A run that was planned and missed counts
against the week, so the app suggests repeating it.

**Stair finisher in gym mode:** in **Settings → Gym sessions**, pick the stair finisher track as a
lifting day's finisher. The gym checklist then shows today's stair session, how many more good
sessions you need before it steps up, and a pain flag. When you tap Finish, the track moves on
once, and the session goes to Hevy as "Stair Machine (Floors)" with your floors and level. The finish
summary shows what the finisher did next to the lift changes, for example "Stair finisher: 5 min continuous
@ level 6 → 6 min continuous @ level 6" or "Hold: 1 more qualifying session to step up", and the next
prescription.

**With Hevy:**
- Walks, runs and home routines you finish in the app are sent to Hevy from the server, just like gym sessions: Walking, Treadmill or Running for cardio, Stretching or Yoga for routines. If Hevy is down, they are retried, and never sent twice.
- If you finish a workout in Hevy instead, the pull reads the time, floors, distance and RPE and moves the track on. A finisher done inside a lifting workout also marks the finisher done in the app.
- A workout pulled twice, or edited in Hevy after it was matched, never counts twice.
- **Settings → Hevy routines → Push routines into Hevy** (on by default) controls whether planned routines are pushed into Hevy. Finished sessions are always sent.

## Coach signals

These only ever suggest. They never change your lifting or cardio numbers.

- **Recovery colour.** The recovery card is gone from the main page; the API (`/readiness`) stays, and gym mode still leaves out the finisher on a red day recorded there.
- **Running gate.** Walk → run can wait behind a gate: trend weight at or under 90 kg, the walk builder at 45 minutes and 8 % incline rated Easy for two weeks, and no knee, shin or Achilles pain for four weeks. (Plan v3 uses the gate without the walk-builder condition.) The Cardio page shows each condition's progress and offer **Start** once they're all met. You decide when to start. On the Cardio page, **Wait for the running gate** adds it to an existing Walk → run track.
- **Phase heads-up.** A plan phase (for example Base, Build, Transition) can list what changes when it starts. The Workout page shows them the week before and on the first day, and a push notification goes out once. If the phase swaps an exercise (Pause Squat → Squat (Barbell)), tap **Swap** and confirm to make the swap in your workout.

## Install on your phone

**iPhone (Safari, iOS 16.4+)**: open the site, tap **Share → Add to Home Screen → Add**, then launch it from
the home screen. Notifications only work from there, and only after you turn them on in Settings.

**Android (Chrome)**: open the site, tap **⋮ → Install app**.

## How it's built

A .NET 10 API (Clean Architecture, CQRS, PostgreSQL) and a React 19 PWA. Its seams:

- **Domain**: the A2S2 workout and its progression strategies, the training plan, cardio protocols, mobility
  routines, habits, body weight and meals. No framework dependencies.
- **Hevy anti-corruption layer**: every call to Hevy goes through the server, with retries and rate-limit handling.
- **Push sender**: a background job that sends each nudge at most once per person per local day.
- **Local time**: all dates are Europe/London local dates, so streaks and week rollover stay correct across clock changes.
